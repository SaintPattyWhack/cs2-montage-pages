Privacy policy

cs2-montage

cs2-montage is a personal tool with exactly one user: its author. It is not distributed, has no other users, no accounts, and no server. This policy describes what it does with data on the one computer it runs on.

What it accesses

  • My own gameplay recordings, created by OBS on my computer, and clips cut from them.
  • My own YouTube channel, to upload videos I made and approved.
  • My own YouTube analytics — view counts, likes, shares, subscribers gained and average view percentage for those videos.
  • Files it created in my own Google Drive, using the drive.file scope, which cannot reach anything else in Drive.

What it does not do

  • It does not collect data about anyone else.
  • It does not send data to any third party, analytics service, or advertiser. The only network destinations are Google's own APIs, acting on my behalf, and the platforms I publish to.
  • It does not sell, share, or transfer data to anyone.
  • It has no other users, so there is no other person's data to hold.

Where data is stored

Everything lives on my own computer, in the project's own folder, and in my own Google account. Access tokens are stored in a local file that is excluded from version control and from backups that leave the machine. No copy is held anywhere I do not control.

TikTok

For my own TikTok account (Bingo Bango Bongo) the tool uses TikTok's Content Posting and Display APIs, with the scopes user.info.basic, video.upload, video.publish and video.list.

What it accesses, and why:

  • The authorised account's basic profile (user.info.basic): to confirm which account I authorised.
  • Posting options for that account (the nickname and the privacy levels and limits TikTok allows it), fetched before a video is posted so I can see and choose the settings on my own review page.
  • The videos I upload (video.upload, video.publish): my own gameplay montages, with captions, sent to my own account either as drafts or, after I approve a specific video and its settings on my review page, as a direct post.
  • My own videos' counts (video.list): the video id, caption, length, and view, like, comment and share counts of videos on my account, so I can see how my own videos perform. Retention figures that TikTok Studio shows me while I am signed in to my own account are recorded too.

Where it is stored: only on my own computer. The TikTok access and refresh tokens are in a local secrets file that is excluded from version control. The cached posting options, my per-video approval records, and the video ids and counts are in local files and a local database. Nothing is copied to a server I run, because there is none.

Sharing: TikTok data is not sold, shared or transferred to anyone, and is not used for advertising. It is only sent back to TikTok's own APIs.

Retention: tokens are kept until I revoke access. Approval records, video ids and counts are kept for as long as I keep my own records of my own videos. There is no one else's TikTok data to retain.

Revoking access: remove this app from the apps connected to my account in the TikTok app's settings (under Security and permissions). TikTok then invalidates its tokens, and the tool can no longer reach the account. To also erase the local copies, I delete the token entry in the local secrets file and the local TikTok files and database rows by hand; the tool has no automatic deletion.

Other people appearing in the footage

Counter-Strike 2's kill feed shows the in-game display names of other players in the same match. Those names appear in published gameplay clips, as they do in ordinary gameplay footage of this game. They are pseudonyms already visible to every player in that match and in public match history. No other information about those players is collected, stored, or derived.

Retention and deletion

Raw recordings are deleted from my computer once clips have been extracted and verified. Published videos and their statistics live in my Google account and are removed by deleting them there. Revoking this application's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions immediately ends its ability to reach any Google data.

Contact

This is a personal project. Enquiries go to the Google account that owns it, which is the support address given on the app's consent screen.
